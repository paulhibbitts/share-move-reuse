**Open both ways:** an open licence gives you the [5Rs](https://opencontent.org/definition/?target=_blank), but content that's open for readers often stays inside the platform for its authors – harder to edit elsewhere, move or keep. Share, Move, Reuse is what makes them practical, for both.

[definition title="The DIY answer: plain Markdown"]
A simple text format for web pages – `**bold**`, `## Heading`, `- list item`. It's supported almost everywhere, from GitHub and note-taking apps to Google Docs, and it's widely used by other tools, including AI. Keep every page in Markdown, on a free site you control.
[/definition]

**Not ready to openly license your work?** Plain Markdown still keeps it yours – it outlasts any one platform, and moves, backs up and adapts easily for years to come.

**Reuse anywhere:** you (and, with an open licence, anyone) can get the Markdown behind any page they're viewing, with its callouts, math and diagrams intact – so it opens as it is in [Docsify-This](https://docsify-this.net/?target=_blank) (which shows Markdown as web pages) or on GitHub.

**See the proof:** [a Pressbooks book](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/universaldesign2/?target=_blank) and [the same book in Open Publishing Space](https://demo.hibbittsdesign.org/variants/grav-open-publishing-quark2-pressbooks-udl/?target=_blank) or [on Docsify-This](https://docsify-this.net/?basePath=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/paulhibbitts/docsify-open-publishing-pressbooks-udl-site/main&homepage=home.md&sidebar=true&loadSidebar=_sidebar.md&loadNavbar=_navbar.md&pagination=true&searchbox=true&collapsibleSidebarGroups=true&collapseSidebarGroups=true&dark-mode=auto&name=Universal%20Design%20for%20Learning%20%28UDL%29&edit-link=https://raw.githubusercontent.com/paulhibbitts/docsify-open-publishing-pressbooks-udl-site/main/home.md&edit-link-text=This%20page%20as%20Markdown&edit-link-emoji=:page_facing_up:&target=_blank), converted with no revisions – more in [From Pressbooks or Your LMS](/guide/modules/from-pressbooks-or-your-lms).

**One course, many uses:** read it at your own pace, present it like slides (← and → move between pages), embed it in an LMS, print it or get its Markdown – all from the same pages. Write once, use it many ways.
